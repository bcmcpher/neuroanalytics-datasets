# 1. Find data

Most CNS projects are secondary analyses of existing open datasets. The work at this
stage is figuring out which dataset can actually answer your question, and starting the
access process early — applications often take weeks, and they run independently of
everything else you are setting up.

## Datasets used in this program

- **[PREVENT-AD](../data/databases/prevent-ad.md)** — longitudinal pre-symptomatic
  Alzheimer's cohort, with imaging, MEG/EEG, PET and CSF. The most thoroughly documented
  dataset in this book, and the one several scholars build on
- **[ADNI](../data/databases/adni.md)** — the long-running Alzheimer's neuroimaging
  initiative
- **[UK Biobank](../data/databases/ukbb.md)** — very large population cohort with imaging
  and genetics
- **[ABCD](../data/databases/abcd.md)** — adolescent brain and cognitive development

## Portals to search

Portals index many datasets rather than hosting one:

- **[OpenNeuro](../data/portals/open-neuro.md)** — the largest open archive of
  BIDS-formatted neuroimaging data
- **[CONP](../data/portals/conp-data-portal.md)** — the Canadian Open Neuroscience
  Platform
- **[BrainCODE](../data/portals/brain-code.md)** — Ontario Brain Institute data,
  including ONDRI
- **[DANDI](../data/portals/dandi.md)** — open neurophysiology
- **[brainlife.io](../data/portals/brainlife.md)** — data plus browser-based pipelines
- **[UK Biobank RAP](../data/portals/ukbb-rap.md)** — the analysis platform you work
  through for UKBB
- **[Nightingale](../data/portals/nightingale.md)**, **[HBI](../data/portals/hbi.md)**

## Choosing

Work backwards from the question. Two practical filters:

- **Does it have the modality you need?** Check [fMRI](../modalities/fmri.md),
  [structural MRI](../modalities/smri.md), [DWI](../modalities/dwi.md),
  [EEG/MEG](../modalities/eeg-meg.md), [PET](../modalities/pet.md)
- **Is it longitudinal?** If your question is about *progression*, you need repeated
  scans, which rules out a lot of otherwise attractive datasets

Or start from someone else's answer: the [research topics](../research_topics/alzheimers.md)
hubs list which scholars work on what, and their pages name the datasets they chose.
