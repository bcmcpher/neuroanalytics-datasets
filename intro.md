# The CNS Scholars Guide

This book documents the open datasets, tools, and methods used by scholars in the
**Canadian Neuroanalytics Scholars Program**. It is written by the scholars themselves,
accumulated over the course of their projects, and intended for the people who come next.

If you are starting a project and wondering where to find data, how to get compute, which
preprocessing pipeline to run, or whether a particular tool is worth the week it will take
to learn — that is what this is for.

**New here? Begin with [Start Here: a research pathway](pathway/overview.md)**, which
walks the same material in the order you will actually need it: find data, get compute,
organize it, preprocess, analyze, share.

## How it is organized

**[Start Here](pathway/overview.md)** — the guided path through everything below, for
anyone who does not yet know what they are looking for.

**[Meet the scholars](cohort1/hao-ting_wang.md)** — what each scholar is working on, and
increasingly, first-hand accounts of the tools they adopted and why. Those testimonial
sections are the part you will not find in any software documentation.

**Databases and Data Portals** — the open datasets scholars actually use, with notes on
access and structure.

**[Research Topics](research_topics/alzheimers.md)** and
**[Modalities](modalities/fmri.md)** — entry points that connect a subject or a data type
to the people working on it and the tools that apply.

**[Research Methods](deep-learning.md)** — the analytical approaches in use across the
program, from deep learning to normative modelling.

**Research Tools** — the practical documentation: git, containers, compute clusters,
BIDS, preprocessing pipelines.

**Discussions** — accumulated hard-won knowledge that does not fit anywhere else, such as
why the same function gives different answers in MATLAB and Python.

## Contributing

This is a growing resource and anyone can add to it. If there is information you want to
see, open a PR.

If you are a scholar adding or updating your own page, see the
[scholar page template](contributing/bio-template.md) — particularly the section on
writing up the tools you adopted, which is the most useful thing you can contribute to
someone arriving after you.
